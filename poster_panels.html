01 · MOTIVATION

Not all AI users are the same, so why give them the same AI?

As AI assistance becomes ubiquitous in decision-making tasks, a key design challenge emerges: uniform support doesn't serve all users equally. Some people over-rely on AI even when it's wrong. Others ignore it even when it's right.

Prior work established that over-reliers are not a monolith — they differ in underlying reasoning ability. We asked: can we exploit that difference to match AI support type to user type?

02 · HYPOTHESIS

Match specificity of AI hint to user's reasoning profile.

We split participants on two axes: logic level (ability to apply feature-based reasoning) and overreliance tendency (following wrong AI recommendations).

Hi Logic · Overrelier (key cell)
Capable but lazy -- general family hint should engage reasoning without spoon-feeding
Lo Logic · Overrelier (key cell)
Can't bridge hint to answer -- needs direct species recommendation
Hi Logic · Non-Overrelier
Already engaged -- any hint helps, ceilings expected
Lo Logic · Non-Overrelier
Disengaged and unable -- intervention value unclear
03 · STUDY

Bird classification task, 2x2 between-subjects design.

Participants identified bird species from images using visual features, aided by AI recommendations that varied in both specificity (family-level vs. species-level) and correctness (wrong, partial, correct).

Study flow Demographics → Attention check → Logic probes → AI probes → Pretest → Main task with intervention → Posttest → Feedback
Key measures Main task accuracy, pre/post learning rate, response time variance, correlation of RT with AI correctness level
04 · FINDINGS

Reliance type didn't move the needle. Logic level did.

Species-level AI outperformed family-level across nearly every group, including groups it theoretically shouldn't have helped most.

ANCOVA Logic × AI support was the dominant interaction. Overreliance classification alone had negligible effect.
T-TEST Low-logic users showed significant species AI advantage (p=0.008). High-logic users showed no meaningful difference between conditions.
AI=0.5 When AI was partially wrong, family support outperformed species — the only condition where family consistently won. Partial species hints actively misled users.
RT DATA Species users slowed down on incorrect AI; family users showed uncorrelated RT variance — possibly navigation friction, not deeper engagement.
05 · OPEN QUESTION

If reasoning capacity is the real moderator, what does that change?

The operative variable wasn't whether someone tends to follow AI -- it was whether they had the capacity to use a hint productively. That reframes the design problem entirely: not "who over-relies" but "who can bridge from a hint to an answer."

Measurement
Can reasoning capacity be inferred from behavioral traces alone, without probe questions? Response patterns, feature selection, and error types might be enough.
Construct validity
Are logic level and overreliance tendency truly orthogonal? The ANCOVA suggests they may not be -- one may be absorbing the other's variance.
Generalizability
Does the reasoning capacity x AI specificity interaction hold beyond classification tasks? What domains or AI modalities would break this pattern?
